Soldering and servo power

Sensor header pins

The PN532 and GY-521 usually ship with their pin strips loose, meant to be soldered on. Check yours: if the pins are already fixed in the board, you're fine.

  • Best: buy versions sold "pre-soldered" or "with headers soldered". Both modules are easy to find that way.
  • Also fine: ask anyone with a soldering iron (a campus makerspace or lab). It's about 10 pins and a few minutes.
  • Test-only stop-gap: push the loose pins through the module's holes, then push the female jumper ends onto them. Tilt the module slightly so the pins press against the holes. It's flaky: if i2c-scan finds a module only sometimes, this is why. Don't build the final model on it.

Servo power

Our build powers the servo from the Uno's 5V (the lab ULA OK'd it for a small MG90S). The capacitor next to the servo covers the short current spikes when it starts moving. If the start-up messages reappear in the serial monitor when the servo moves, or the sensors drop out, the Uno is browning out: switch to a separate 5 V supply (step 12 explains the swap).

If you need a separate supply (no solder)

  • 5 V, 2 A wall adapter + a barrel-jack-to-screw-terminal adapter. Clamp a jumper into each terminal (+ and −).
  • 4×AA holder with rechargeable NiMH batteries (about 4.8 V). Twist stranded leads tight before pushing them into the board. Four fresh alkalines give about 6.4 V, which is over the MG90S's 6 V limit.
  • Avoid breadboard power modules that plug into the rails. In this layout the rails belong to the Uno's 5V.
  • It must say 5V DC and at least 2 A. Not 9 V or 12 V.

    Getting parts onto the model

    • LEDs and buzzer: push each leg into the female end of a female–male jumper, then plug the male ends into the same holes the part used. Note which jumper is on the long leg. Wrap the leg joints in tape so they can't touch each other.
    • Light tube: push the tube ends over the LEDs and seal with heat-shrink (a hair dryer or lighter shrinks it, no solder involved).
    • Servo: if it sits far from the board, use a servo extension cable or three longer jumpers.
    • Sensor wires: keep SDA/SCL as short as you can, and away from the buzzer wires. If i2c-scan gets unreliable after moving things, long wires are the first suspect.
    • Most breadboards have a sticky back for fixing the board inside the model.
    Before you plug in USB

    Pre-power check

    After power-up, both sensor power LEDs should be on. The startup log should say reader=OK accel=OK, and i2c-scan should show 0x68 (MPU-6050). The reader is on UART, so it won't appear in the scan.
